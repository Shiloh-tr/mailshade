## Fixtures

- `color-matrix.html`: solid surface inversion anchors.

- `gradient-matrix.html`: linear, radial, repeating, layered, and transparent gradients.
- `dark-native-holdout.html`: a naturally dark campaign with nested light and saturated surfaces.

## Findings

- Gmail iOS transforms the same source color differently according to its foreground/background role and local text polarity.
- Authored CSS gradients were preserved at the sampled points while foreground text colors were transformed.
- Naturally dark email structure was not simply preserved: solid surfaces and light text could still invert.
- The simulator therefore preserves gradient declarations, applies role-specific anchors, and uses text-polarity-aware residual interpolation for unseen solid colors.

## Holdout gate

The gradient holdout passed visual and sampled-pixel comparison. On the naturally dark holdout, the two contextual solid surfaces used for the activation gate were:

| Region | Gmail iOS | Simulator | ΔE76 |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Nested pale notice | `#2a2507` | `#2d2607` | 1.5621 |
| Nested green CTA | `#0b3f24` | `#103a28` | 6.8380 |

The v0.1 draft gate was ΔE76 ≤ 7 for sampled contextual surfaces. Both passed. This validates the current fixture set; it does not imply pixel-perfect coverage of every Gmail iOS build or arbitrary email construction.

The later strict validator re-ran these two regions as an independent holdout and marked the result release-gate eligible: median ΔE00 `1.3540`, p95 ΔE00 `3.6767`. The color gate therefore passes. A subsequent dedicated typography run disproved the earlier auto-sizing interpretation: paired Gmail light/dark wrapping is unchanged and normalized geometry passes at a maximum 2 px delta.
